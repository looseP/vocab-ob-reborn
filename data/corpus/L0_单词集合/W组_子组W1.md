# W组 · 子组W1（L1 生产词条）

## want

### Identity
- lemma: want
- title: want
- pos: v/n
- ipa: /wɒnt/
- cefr: A1
- aliases: wants, wanted, wanting, unwanted

### Short Definition
想要；需要；缺乏；缺乏之物

### Core Definitions
1. 想要；希望得到或做某事
   - en: to desire or wish for something or to do something
   - priority: 1
   - tags: A
2. 需要；缺乏
   - en: to need something or be lacking or deficient in something
   - priority: 2
   - tags: B
3. 缺乏；不足、需求之物
   - en: a state of deficiency or something that is lacking and needed
   - priority: 3
   - tags: B

### Prototype
want = 米缸空了，孩子还扯着衣角

### Morphology
- prefix:
- root: want / Old Norse *vanta* (be lacking, want)
- suffix:
- family: want, wanted, wanting, unwanted, wane

### Etymology Narrative

源自古 Norse 语 vanta（缺乏、需要），其日耳曼语与 PIE 链条关涉“离去、耗尽、不足”。画面是某物从手里失落、剩下个空缺。“想要、盼望、觉得需要”一义由“缺少某物”这一状态发展而来：由客观的匮乏转为主观的需求与欲望。名词保留了较古的“缺乏”义，后来也指“想要的东西”。
### Mnemonic
- type: story
- text: Imagine an empty shelf: what is lacking is what you want, so “缺乏” naturally leads to “需要、想要.”

### Semantic Chain
欠缺 -> 缺乏 -> 有所需 -> 想要

### Reviewer Notes
- Etymonline: https://www.etymonline.com/word/want；verb c. 1200 *wanten* “be lacking, be deficient in something” from Old Norse *vanta*; “desire, wish for, feel the need of” is recorded by 1706; noun c. 1200 “deficiency, insufficiency, absence or lack of.”
- This entry follows the source-backed lack → need/desire path. It does not treat `want` as an English `w-` prefix formation and does not merge it with `wont`; the source instead links the historical lack sense with `wane`.
- `-ed`, `-ing`, and `un-` are not analyzed as the historical morphology of the lemma; the root is kept conservative.
- confidence: source-backed

## way

### Identity
- lemma: way
- title: way
- pos: n/adv/int
- ipa: /weɪ/
- cefr: A1
- aliases: ways, wayward, highway, pathway

### Short Definition
道路；路径；方法；方向

### Core Definitions
1. 道路；路径、行进路线
   - en: a road, path, or course by which a place can be reached
   - priority: 1
   - tags: A
2. 方法；方式
   - en: a method or manner of doing or experiencing something
   - priority: 2
   - tags: B
3. 方向；路程、生活方式
   - en: a direction, distance, or course of life
   - priority: 3
   - tags: B

### Prototype
way = 山那边有条小道，半天能到

### Morphology
- prefix:
- root: way / Old English *weg* (track, path, course of travel)
- suffix:
- family: way, ways, away, highway, pathway, weigh, wagon

### Etymology Narrative

经中古英语 wei 溯自古英语 weg（可通达某处的路途或小径）。画面是林间踩出的那条小道。由出行所经的实际路径，引申为人生道路、方法或方式，以及方向。其日耳曼语历史通过那个关涉“行走、移动、运送”的 PIE 词根与 weigh 相连。
### Mnemonic
- type: image
- text: 古英语 weg，林间踩出来的那条小道 → 从脚下走的路，转成办事的法子与要去的地方。

### Semantic Chain
路 -> 行程所循 -> 指向一方 -> 道路

### Reviewer Notes
- Etymonline: https://www.etymonline.com/word/way；Middle English *wei* from Old English *weg* “track or path by which some place may be reached, line or course of travel, distance to be travelled.”
- The source records the path → course of life → method/manner → direction chain. `way` is not split into a modern English prefix or suffix.
- The source connects *way* and *weigh* through PIE *wegh-* “to go, move, transport in a vehicle”; this is a historical family link, not a claim that modern `way` contains a productive affix.
- confidence: source-backed

## weak

### Identity
- lemma: weak
- title: weak
- pos: adj
- ipa: /wiːk/
- cefr: A2
- aliases: weaker, weakest, weakly, weakness, weaken

### Short Definition
虚弱的；薄弱的；无力的

### Core Definitions
1. 虚弱的；缺乏身体力量的
   - en: lacking bodily strength or physical power
   - priority: 1
   - tags: A
2. 薄弱的；脆弱的、效果不足的
   - en: lacking strength, effectiveness, or resistance to damage
   - priority: 2
   - tags: B
3. 无说服力的；不擅长的、疲软的
   - en: lacking force, skill, authority, or economic strength
   - priority: 3
   - tags: C

### Prototype
weak = 像柔软、易弯曲的东西一样，在外力面前容易屈服，因而“扛不住”。

### Morphology
- prefix:
- root: weak / Old Norse *veikr* (lacking strength)
- suffix:
- family: weak, weaker, weakest, weakly, weakness, weaken, weakling

### Etymology Narrative

源自古 Norse 语 veikr（缺乏力气的），与古英语 wac（柔弱的、易弯的、软的）同源。日耳曼语的意义构拟中含有“屈让、退让”之意。画面是一根软枝条一压就弯。由柔软或易弯的物理意象，推及身体的虚弱、物件的脆薄、论证的无力，以及权力的有限。
### Mnemonic
- type: image
- text: 古诺尔斯语 veikr，与古英语 wac（柔软、易弯）同源，含“屈让”之意 → 一根软枝一压就弯，抵挡不住。

### Semantic Chain
柔软易弯 -> 受力即屈 -> 力气不足 -> 虚弱

### Reviewer Notes
- Etymonline: https://www.etymonline.com/word/weak；c. 1300 *weik* “deficient in bodily strength; exhausted from exertion” from Old Norse *veikr* “lacking strength,” cognate with Old English *wac*.
- The source traces the history through Proto-Germanic *waika-* “yield” and PIE *weik-* “to bend, to wind,” supporting the conservative “yield/weakness” chain.
- `weak` and `week` are not treated as a false English pair: the page gives a Germanic cognate chain where Dutch *week* means “weak, soft,” but the modern English spelling similarity alone is not used as a semantic link.
- prefix and suffix remain blank; no modern `w-` or `-eak` analysis is made.
- confidence: source-backed

## wear

### Identity
- lemma: wear
- title: wear
- pos: v/n
- ipa: /weə/
- cefr: A2
- aliases: wears, wore, worn, wearing, wearable, wearout

### Short Definition
穿戴；磨损；消耗；衣物；磨损

### Core Definitions
1. 穿；戴、使某物覆盖在身上
   - en: to have clothing or something else on the body
   - priority: 1
   - tags: A
2. 磨损；因持续使用而变旧或消耗
   - en: to become damaged, thinner, or less effective through continued use
   - priority: 2
   - tags: B
3. 衣物；磨损或损耗
   - en: clothing or the action or result of wearing and deterioration
   - priority: 3
   - tags: B

### Prototype
wear = 他把外套披上出了门；鞋底磨得只剩薄薄一层

### Morphology
- prefix:
- root: wear / Old English *werian* (clothe, put on, cover up)
- suffix:
- family: wear, wore, worn, wearing, wearable, wearout, footwear

### Etymology Narrative

穿衣义的动词源自古英语 werian（穿衣、穿上、遮蔽），后来“因使用而损耗”一义出自衣物久穿所生的效果。另有一个同形的日耳曼语动词，意为“阻止、挡开、防护”，故“穿着／佩带”一线与“防御”一线不可混为一谈。现代 wear 作“磨损、耐久、消耗”讲，仍属“持续使用”这一线。
### Mnemonic
- type: image
- text: 古英语 werian（穿衣、遮蔽）；另有一支同形动词意为“阻挡、防护”，两线不可混 → 衣裳日复一日覆在身上，久了也就磨薄。

### Semantic Chain
披覆于身 -> 着衣在身 -> 久久使用 -> 穿、穿戴

### Reviewer Notes
- Etymonline: https://www.etymonline.com/word/wear；clothing verb Middle English *weren* from Old English *werian* “to clothe, put on, cover up”; “consume by frequent or habitual use” is from the effect of continued use on clothes.
- The page separately notes homonymous Germanic forms for “prevent, ward off, protect” (Gothic *warjan*, Old English *werian*). Clothing/carry, wear-away/erode, and endure are not presented as one undifferentiated source line.
- Do not merge `wear` with `wary` or `were` by spelling. The entry keeps prefix and suffix blank because the source does not support a productive modern affix analysis.
- confidence: uncertain

## weigh

### Identity
- lemma: weigh
- title: weigh
- pos: v/n
- ipa: /weɪ/
- cefr: B1
- aliases: weighs, weighed, weighing, weigh-in, weighable

### Short Definition
称重；有……重；权衡；有分量

### Core Definitions
1. 称量重量；有……重
   - en: to measure the weight of something or have a particular weight
   - priority: 1
   - tags: A
2. 权衡；认真考虑
   - en: to consider or examine something carefully before forming an opinion
   - priority: 2
   - tags: B
3. 有分量；产生影响
   - en: to have importance or influence
   - priority: 3
   - tags: B

### Prototype
weigh = 先移动、举起并称量重物，再把选项放到心里的“天平”上比较。

### Morphology
- prefix:
- root: weigh / Old English *wegan* (move, carry, weigh)
- suffix:
- family: weigh, weighed, weighing, weigh-in, weight, way, wagon

### Etymology Narrative

源自古英语 wegan（称……的分量、度量），亦作“举起、搬运、支撑、承受、移动”。历史路径是：由“移动”到“举起、托起”，以便秤出重量；画面是把东西托在手里掂分量。而物理上的称重又比喻为权衡、考量各种选项。它与 way、weight 的关系是历史上的同源关系，不是对现代拼写部件的拆分。
### Mnemonic
- type: image
- text: 古英语 wegan，先有“移动、举起”，再为秤重而托起 → 把东西托在手里掂分量；掂量主意也是它。

### Semantic Chain
挪动 -> 提起称量 -> 定其分量 -> 称量

### Reviewer Notes
- Etymonline: https://www.etymonline.com/word/weigh；Middle English *weien* from Old English *wegan* “find the weight of, measure,” also “lift, carry, support, sustain, bear; move.”
- The source gives the prehistoric movement → lifting → bearing up to determine weight path and dates figurative “consider, ponder, examine” to the mid-14th century.
- Etymonline links the history to PIE *wegh-* “to go, move, transport in a vehicle,” supporting the historical relation to `way`; `weight` is the noun family. `-igh` is not treated as a suffix.
- confidence: source-backed

## weight

### Identity
- lemma: weight
- title: weight
- pos: n/v
- ipa: /weɪt/
- cefr: A2
- aliases: weights, weighted, weighting, weighty, weightless, overweight

### Short Definition
重量；体重；分量；负担；加权

### Core Definitions
1. 重量；体重
   - en: how heavy someone or something is
   - priority: 1
   - tags: A
2. 重物；砝码、负担
   - en: a heavy object or something that presses down physically or mentally
   - priority: 2
   - tags: C
3. 分量；重要性、影响力
   - en: importance, influence, or significance understood as having weight
   - priority: 3
   - tags: B

### Prototype
weight = 他扛着那袋米上秤，指针一下子压到了底

### Morphology
- prefix:
- root: weight / Old English *wiht*, *gewiht* (weighing, downward force of a body)
- suffix:
- family: weight, weights, weighted, weighting, weighty, weightless, overweight, weigh

### Etymology Narrative

源自中古英语 weght 与古英语 wiht、gewiht（称重、物体的下坠之力、重的物理属性）。画面是把东西托在手里感受它的沉。由这种物理之力，发展出“重物”“负担”，再引申为比喻上的重要性与影响力。其词族与 weigh 相连，但词尾的拼写并非现代的后缀。
### Mnemonic
- type: contrast
- text: weigh 是掂这个动作，weight 是掂出来的那份沉 → 手心觉到的那股下坠之力，也可指心头的担子。

### Semantic Chain
称重 -> 坠下之力 -> 沉甸甸 -> 重量

### Reviewer Notes
- Etymonline: https://www.etymonline.com/word/weight；Middle English *weght* from Old English *wiht, gewiht* “weighing, downward force of a body, physical property of heaviness,” from Proto-Germanic *wihti-*, from *weg-*.
- The source records “piece of metal used on account of its weight,” “burden,” and later “importance,” supporting the physical → abstract semantic chain.
- `weight`, `weigh`, and historical *wiegh* forms are treated as a family through the source’s link to `weigh`; `-ght` is not treated as a modern suffix, and no English `w-` prefix is assigned.
- confidence: source-backed

## widespread

### Identity
- lemma: widespread
- title: widespread
- pos: adj
- ipa: /ˈwaɪdspred/
- cefr: B2
- aliases: wide-spread, widespreadness, widespreadly

### Short Definition
分布广的；普遍的；流传广的

### Core Definitions
1. 分布广的；遍及许多地方的
   - en: diffused over a great distance or occurring in many places
   - priority: 1
   - tags: A
2. 广泛的；普遍存在或流传的
   - en: existing, affecting, or being accepted across a broad area or among many people
   - priority: 2
   - tags: C

### Prototype
widespread = 在宽广范围内铺展开来，因而覆盖很多地方或很多人。

### Morphology
- prefix:
- root: wide + spread / Old English *wid* + spread from *sprædan* (broad + stretch out, diffuse)
- suffix:
- family: widespread, wide-spread, widespreadness, widespreadly, wide, spread

### Etymology Narrative

本词是英语复合词，1705年作 wide-spread，由 wide 加 spread 的过去分词构成。字面意象是某物铺展在广大的面积上，由此发展出“在许多地方发生”与“为许多人所接受或经历”两义。wide 是英语的词汇成分，并非拉丁语前缀。
### Mnemonic
- type: image
- text: Imagine paint spread across a wide wall: wide + spread gives “铺展很广,” hence widespread.

### Semantic Chain
宽阔 -> 散及一片 -> 处处可见 -> 广泛

### Reviewer Notes
- Etymonline: https://www.etymonline.com/word/widespread；also *wide-spread*, “diffused to a great distance, occurring in many places,” 1705, from `wide` + past participle of `spread`; earlier *wide-spreading* is recorded from the 1590s.
- The source’s direction is the English compound `wide + spread`: broad spatial extent plus spreading or diffusion. `wide` is not treated as a Latin prefix, and no modern affix is assigned to the whole compound.
- The linked component histories support Old English *wid* for `wide` and Old English *sprædan* for `spread`; the entry remains a transparent English compound rather than a Latin-derived morphology analysis.
- confidence: source-backed
